Quick Actions

Use this guide when you have an issue at work. Always contact a steward first for formal representation.

Important Reminders

  • Request a steward before any meeting that could lead to discipline.
  • Document everything — dates, times, witnesses, what was said.
  • Grievance timelines are short (often 5–10 working days). Do not wait.
  • Verify any company claim (points, rates, rules) with your steward before accepting it.
  • This tool is for information only. Your steward and the CBA control your rights.

How to Address a Workplace Issue

Follow these steps from the moment something happens until you get help. Act quickly — grievance timelines are short.

1. Document immediately
Write down the date, time, what happened, who was involved, what was said, and any witnesses. Take photos of any written notice or form. Keep your own copy.
2. If management calls you into a meeting
Ask what the meeting is about. If it could lead to discipline or affect your working conditions, read the Weingarten statement and request a steward. Do not answer questions until the steward is present and you have had time to consult. Do not sign anything until you understand it and have spoken with a steward.
3. Contact a steward right away
Call or text Senior Steward Vincent Gonzalez (530) 908-4849, Lester Burden (916) 804-9682, or Joe Gonzales (916) 856-0785. Give them the facts, the date of the incident, and any documents. Do this within a day or two — do not wait.
4. Request the steward file a grievance if needed
The steward decides whether a grievance is appropriate and timely under Article XXIII. Provide them everything you have. Use the Grievance Note generator in this app if you want a draft to send them.
5. Follow the grievance steps
Step One discussion with the supervisor should happen no later than 5 working days after the event. Written steps follow with short deadlines. Your steward handles the formal process.
6. Keep tracking and stay calm
Continue to document any further events. Do not discuss the details of the issue with coworkers in a way that could create problems. Follow any instructions from your steward.
Remember: The steward and the contract are your protection. This app helps you prepare and stay organized, but the steward is the one who represents you.

Attendance / Tardy Issue Checklist

  • Ask for a written list of every occurrence and the exact dates.
  • Check your hire date — the rolling 12-month period can only count time after you started.
  • Verify each point matches the policy chart (tardy = 0.5, no-call = 1, etc.).
  • Note any exceptions that should not have been charged (FMLA, job injury, approved leave, Union business, etc.).
  • Ask whether other employees with similar records received earlier coaching or different treatment.
  • Request the steward review for just cause (Article XXI) and consistent application.
  • Do not accept the point total without verification.

What Not to Do

  • Do not sign any warning, form, or statement until a steward has reviewed it (or sign only “received, not agreed” if the steward advises).
  • Do not answer investigatory questions without a steward present.
  • Do not discuss the details of your case with management or coworkers without your steward’s guidance.
  • Do not wait days or weeks to contact a steward — timelines are short.
  • Do not assume the company’s point total or version of events is accurate.
  • Do not waive any right under the contract or Weingarten.

Union Rules & Member Rights

Key rights and responsibilities for Teamsters Local 150 members at FRC-PIRMI. These come from the IBT Constitution, the LMRDA (Labor-Management Reporting and Disclosure Act), and your CBA. Always contact a steward or the Business Agent for official guidance.

Your Core Rights as a Member

  • Equal rights to participate in union meetings, vote, and run for office (subject to reasonable rules).
  • Freedom of speech and assembly within the union.
  • The right to a fair hearing before any discipline by the union.
  • Protection against improper discipline or retaliation for exercising your rights.
  • The right to copies of the CBA and union constitution upon request.
  • Weingarten rights during investigatory meetings with management.

Protected Concerted Activity

You have the right to act together with other employees (or alone on behalf of the group) to improve wages, hours, and working conditions. This includes discussing issues with coworkers, bringing concerns to the steward, and participating in union activities. Management cannot retaliate for protected concerted activity.

Dues & Union Security

Membership requires timely payment of dues. Failure to pay can result in loss of good standing. Contact Kim Chang at Local 150 for dues questions. Good standing is required to vote, run for office, and receive full union representation in some matters.

Stewards & Representation

Stewards are your first line of representation. They have the right to investigate grievances, accompany you in Weingarten meetings, and process grievances under Article XXIII. You should contact a steward promptly when an issue arises.

No-Strike Obligation

The CBA prohibits strikes, slowdowns, and work stoppages during its term. Any alleged violation is subject to the grievance procedure and just cause. Do not engage in unauthorized work stoppages. Channel disputes through the steward and the grievance process.

How to Stay Involved

  • Attend membership meetings.
  • Read the CBA and ask questions.
  • Support other members by directing them to stewards when they have issues.
  • Report any problems with representation to the Business Agent or higher if necessary.
Bottom line: The union exists to protect you. Use your rights, document issues, and work through your steward. The contract and the grievance procedure are your strongest tools.

Weingarten Rights

If a discussion could lead to discipline or affect your working conditions, read this:

“If this discussion could in any way lead to my being disciplined or terminated, or affect my personal working condition, I respectfully request that my union representative or steward be present at the meeting. Without representation, I choose not to answer any questions. I further request reasonable time to consult with my union representative regarding the subject and purpose of the meeting. I shall not consent to any searches or tests affecting my person, property, or effects without first consulting with my union representative.”

You have the right to a steward. Management must allow reasonable time to consult. Do not waive this right.

Grievance Procedure (Article XXIII)

A grievance is any dispute over the meaning, interpretation, or application of the Agreement. Working days exclude Saturdays, Sundays, and holidays. Time limits may be extended by mutual consent.

Step One – Resolution
Discuss with immediate supervisor (alone or with Steward) as soon as possible, no later than 5 working days after the event.
Written Grievance
If not resolved, a written document is prepared and delivered within the specified time limits (commonly 10 working days in later steps).
Step Two / Step Three
Employer responds within set working days. Business Agent may request a meeting. Parties meet and respond within the deadlines.
Step Four – Board of Adjustment
Two members selected by the Company, two by the Union. Majority vote decides. Board has no authority to change the Agreement.
Step Five – Arbitration
Either party may request arbitration in writing within 30 calendar days. Mutually selected arbitrator from an FMCS list of seven. Decision is final and binding.
Act quickly. Contact your steward as soon as an issue arises. Do not miss the early deadlines.

FRC-PIRMI Collective Bargaining Agreement

June 13, 2022 – June 13, 2027. Summaries of key articles based on the contract pages shared. Always refer to the full official document.

Article I – Definitions

Covers operations at 845 Kentucky Ave., Woodland, CA. “Employee” means production and maintenance employees (excluding office clerical, guards, professional, and supervisory personnel).

Article II – Union Recognition and Cooperation

Employer recognizes the Union as the sole bargaining representative for the unit. The Union has the right to represent members on all terms and conditions of employment. Any performance or attendance expectations must be applied consistently and are subject to just cause and the grievance procedure.

Article III – Management Rights

The Company retains certain rights to manage the business, but those rights are limited by the Agreement. Discipline must be for just cause. Rules and changes can be grieved. New or changed jobs require meeting and bargaining with the Union. The Union receives copies of rules.

Article IV – No Strike / No Lockout

No strikes, slowdowns, or work stoppages during the term. Any discipline for an alleged unauthorized stoppage is subject to the grievance procedure and just cause. No lockouts.

Article V – Non-Discrimination

No unlawful discrimination (including Union membership). Compliance with CFRA, FMLA, and ADA reasonable accommodation.

Article VI – Seniority

Seniority is a contractual right that governs job assignment, promotion, layoff, and recall when skill, qualifications, experience, dependability, and ability are equal. Continuous service starts with the latest full-time hire date. The Union can grieve any claim that seniority was improperly ignored. Temporary/seasonal employees are laid off first.

Article VII – Temporary and Seasonal Employees

May be used for limited-term needs. They have no seniority and their terminations are not subject to the grievance procedure. If reclassified to regular, seniority dates from reclassification. New regular hires acquire plant seniority after 120 working days; that probation period is contractual and can be enforced by the Union.

Article VIII – Safety and Health

Employees are expected to follow safety policies. Safety issues, unsafe conditions, or inconsistent enforcement of safety rules can be raised with a steward and grieved under the contract.

Article IX – Hours of Work and Overtime

Normal schedule is typically an 8-hour day / 40-hour week. Reporting pay (4 hours) and callback minimums (4 hours at time-and-a-half) are contractual protections. Overtime is generally 1½× after 8 straight-time hours/day or 40/week (double time in some cases after 12 hours) and is assigned within the department by seniority when skill, ability, and qualifications are equal. Two 10-minute breaks and one 30-minute paid meal for shifts over 5 hours. Changes that affect working conditions can be raised with the Union.

Article X – Vacations

1 week after 1 year, 2 weeks starting year 2, 3 weeks at 5 years, 4 weeks at 15 years. Must work 1,250 hours for full entitlement. Preference by seniority. Day-at-a-time option available. Sell-back of up to 40 hours possible.

Article XI – Leave

Sick leave (updated policy provides no less than 40 hours per year, paid out if unused). Bereavement up to 3 days for immediate family. Jury duty difference pay. Union officials retain seniority on leave.

Article XIII – Subcontracting

The Company is restricted from contracting out work customarily performed by bargaining-unit employees on the premises. The listed exceptions (lack of skill/experience, tools unavailable, time limits, or insufficient numbers) are limited and can be challenged through the grievance procedure if the Union believes they are being misused.

Article XIX – Wages

Wage rates by classification and year are set in Exhibit A and are contractual. Shift differentials: Swing $0.30, Midnight $0.40. Any claim that a duty is not reasonable or that the correct rate was not paid can be grieved.

Article XX – Holidays

Paid holidays include New Year’s Day, President’s Day, Memorial Day, Independence Day, Labor Day, Thanksgiving, Day After Thanksgiving, Christmas Eve, Christmas, New Year’s Eve, plus two personal holidays. Unworked holiday pay requires working scheduled days before and after (or excused).

Article XXI – Discharge

Non-probationary employees must receive written notice stating the reason(s) for any discipline or discharge. The employee has the right to grieve under Article XXIII. Discipline must meet just cause standards.

Article XXII – Representation

Up to four stewards. Names provided in writing to the Company. Regional representatives may be called in. Designated bulletin board for official Union notices.

Article XXIV – Duration

Effective June 13, 2022 through June 12, 2027, then year-to-year unless either party gives 60 days’ written notice to amend, modify, or terminate.

These are plain-language summaries only. The full written CBA controls. Contact a steward for official interpretation.

Union Dues & Benefits

Your dues fund the representation, contract enforcement, and services that protect your job and working conditions. Contact Kim Chang at the Local for your exact amount or any dues questions.

How Dues Are Calculated & Taken Out

  • Local 150 regular monthly dues are calculated as 2.5 times your base hourly rate.
  • According to federal filings, there is a minimum of about $264 per year and a maximum of about $2,472 per year, plus possible additional working dues ($312–$420 per year in some cases).
  • New members may also pay a one-time initiation fee (historically in the $200–$325 range).
  • Dues are normally deducted automatically from your paycheck (check-off) and remitted to the Local once you are a member in good standing.
  • You must stay current on dues to remain in good standing. Good standing is generally required to vote, run for office, and receive full representation rights in some matters.

Example: At a $32/hour base rate, monthly dues are approximately $80 before any adjustments or additional assessments. Confirm your exact amount with Kim Chang at the Local — rates can vary and must be verified.

Membership Requirements

  • Complete any required application or initiation process with the Local.
  • Pay initiation fee (if applicable) and stay current on monthly dues.
  • Maintain good standing by paying dues on time.
  • Members in good standing have the right to participate in meetings, vote, and access full representation.

For the current initiation fee, exact dues scale, or membership questions, contact the Local office or Kim Chang at (916) 392-7070 ext. 38.

Common Scenarios

Quick guidance for frequent issues. Always contact a steward for representation.

Called into an Investigatory Meeting

Ask what the meeting is about. If it could lead to discipline, read the Weingarten statement and request a steward. Do not answer questions until the steward is present. Do not sign anything until reviewed.

Received a Written Warning

Document the date and content. Contact a steward the same day. Request they review for just cause, proper counting of points, and consistency. Use the Grievance Note generator if helpful. Do not wait — timelines are short.

Overtime Denied or Improperly Assigned

OT is generally assigned within the department by seniority when skill, ability, and qualifications are equal. Document the date, who was assigned, and your seniority. Raise it with a steward promptly so it can be grieved if needed.

Unsafe Condition

Report it immediately to your supervisor and a steward. Document the hazard, date, and who you told. Safety issues and inconsistent enforcement can be grieved. Do not continue working if the condition presents an immediate serious risk.

Workplace Injury

If you are hurt at work:

  • Get medical attention immediately if needed. Tell the provider it is a work-related injury.
  • Report the injury to your supervisor as soon as possible and request that an incident report be completed.
  • Document everything — date, time, how it happened, witnesses, and any unsafe conditions that contributed.
  • Contact a steward the same day. Safety issues and how the company handles injuries can be grieved.
  • You may have workers’ compensation rights. Do not sign any statements or releases without understanding them and, when appropriate, speaking with a steward or the Business Agent.
  • Job-related injuries are generally not charged as attendance occurrences under the absenteeism policy.

Do not continue working if you are injured or if the condition remains unsafe. Contact a steward right away.

Attendance Points Disputed

Ask for the written list of every occurrence and date. Verify your hire date (rolling 12-month window starts after you began). Check for exceptions. Contact a steward to review the count and just cause before accepting the total.

Safety & Golden Rules

Violations of the Golden Rules can lead to immediate serious discipline up to termination. All safety issues can be raised with a steward and grieved.

Golden Safety Rules

  • Lockout / Tagout — Never bypass or work on equipment without proper lockout.
  • Confined Space — Follow all entry procedures and permits.
  • Fall Protection — Use required fall protection above the specified height.
  • Elevators — Do not use freight elevators for passenger transport unless authorized.
  • Hot Work — Obtain and follow hot-work permits.
  • Smoke Free — No smoking except in designated areas.

Other Key Rules

  • Hard hat and required PPE at all times in production areas.
  • Report all injuries and near-misses immediately.
  • Do not operate equipment you are not trained and authorized to use.
  • Keep work areas clean; correct or report hazards.
If a condition is unsafe, stop, report it, and contact a steward. Do not accept pressure to ignore safety rules. If you are injured, report it immediately, seek medical care, document everything, and call a steward the same day. Job-related injuries are generally not charged as attendance occurrences.

Rest Break & Meal Period Rules

These are contractual and California labor standards. Denial or interruption of breaks can be grieved.

  • Rest breaks: Two paid 10-minute breaks for shifts over 5 hours (generally one in the first half, one in the second half).
  • Meal period: One 30-minute paid meal for shifts over 5 hours. Clock out and back in as required.
  • Second meal: If the shift exceeds 10 hours, a second meal period may apply unless waived in writing.
  • You are entitled to take your full break/meal time. Returning late is tracked under the attendance policy — plan accordingly.
  • If a break is denied, interrupted, or you are pressured not to take it, document it and tell a steward the same day.
Breaks are a protected right. Do not waive them under pressure. Contact a steward if they are denied.

Drug & Alcohol Testing Triggers

Summary of the plant policy. Marijuana is treated as illegal under federal law even if legal in California. Testing and discipline remain subject to just cause and the grievance procedure.

When testing may occur

  • Post-offer (pre-employment) for new hires.
  • Reasonable suspicion (observable signs of impairment).
  • Post-incident or after certain accidents.
  • Return-to-duty or follow-up under a last-chance agreement.

Member protections

  • You may request a steward if the discussion could lead to discipline.
  • Prescription medication: disclose as required; a valid prescription is generally a defense if used as directed.
  • Positive tests and resulting discipline can be grieved under just cause.
  • Do not consent to a test or sign any acknowledgment without understanding it and, when appropriate, speaking with a steward.
If asked to test, contact a steward immediately. Document the circumstances of the request.
